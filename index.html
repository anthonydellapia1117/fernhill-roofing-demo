Flat roof

Where you see it
Rowhomes and twins all over the city. From the street you only see the cornice. Up top it's a low-slope membrane, usually painted silver.
What goes wrong
The silver coat chalks and cracks, seams open at the parapet, and water ponds in low spots until it finds a way through.
What we do
Reseal the seams and flashing, recoat, or put on a new membrane when the old one is done. We tell you which, and why.

Where flat roofs leak

The stain on your ceiling is often feet away from where the water got in. It runs along the deck first. These five spots are where we look.

  1. Coping on the parapet. Cracked or loose coping lets water into the top of the wall.
  2. Flashing at the parapet. Where the roof turns up the wall. A common leak point once the seal lets go.
  3. Seam over the party wall. Your roof meets your neighbor's here. Two roofs done in different years often meet badly.
  4. Low spot. Water that sits for days works into the seams. It leaves a ring of dirt after it dries.
  5. Scupper and downspout. A clogged outlet backs water onto the roof and over the edge of the membrane.
